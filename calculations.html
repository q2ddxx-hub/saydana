Seven calculation patterns, worked step by step

Calculations appear in three places in the blueprint: compounding (2.6.3), patient assessment (4.5.6) and therapeutic decisions (4.6.2). Almost all of them are one of seven patterns. Learn the pattern, not the question.

Test yourself on the free diagnostic

Before the patterns: three habits that save marks

  • Write the units first. Most lost marks are unit slips, not arithmetic. If your answer comes out in g when the options are in mL, stop and re-read.
  • Estimate before you calculate. You have about 72 seconds a question. A rough answer tells you instantly which options are impossible.
  • Re-read what is being asked. Per dose or per day? The stock or the final product? The dose or the volume that delivers it?

1. Percentage strength

The rule. A percentage is always parts per hundred: % w/v is grams per 100 mL, % w/w is grams per 100 g, % v/v is mL per 100 mL.

Worked example. How much sodium chloride is in 250 mL of a 0.9% w/v infusion?

  • 0.9% w/v means 0.9 g in 100 mL.
  • 250 mL is 2.5 times 100 mL.
  • 0.9 g x 2.5 = 2.25 g.

The trap: reading % w/v as grams per litre. It is per 100 mL.

2. Ratio strength

The rule. 1:1000 w/v means 1 g in 1000 mL, which is 1 mg per mL. To convert a ratio to a percentage, divide: 1 in 1000 is 0.1%.

Worked example. A 1:200 w/v solution: how many mg are in 5 mL?

  • 1:200 w/v is 1 g in 200 mL, so 1000 mg in 200 mL.
  • That is 5 mg per mL.
  • 5 mL x 5 mg/mL = 25 mg.

The trap: the bigger the second number, the weaker the solution. 1:5000 is weaker than 1:1000.

3. Dilution

The rule. The drug does not change, only the volume does: C1 x V1 = C2 x V2.

Worked example. How much 5% w/v stock is needed to make 120 mL of a 1.5% w/v solution?

  • V1 = (C2 x V2) / C1 = (1.5 x 120) / 5.
  • = 180 / 5 = 36 mL of stock.
  • Add diluent to 120 mL, so 120 - 36 = 84 mL of diluent.

The trap: answering with the diluent volume when the question asked for the stock, or the reverse.

4. Alligation

The rule. Mixing a stronger and a weaker preparation to hit a target strength. The parts of each are the differences across the diagonal.

Worked example. Make 500 g of an 8% w/w ointment from a 20% w/w and a 5% w/w ointment.

  • Parts of the 20%: target minus weaker = 8 - 5 = 3 parts.
  • Parts of the 5%: stronger minus target = 20 - 8 = 12 parts.
  • Total 15 parts, so one part = 500 g / 15 = 33.33 g.
  • 20% ointment: 3 x 33.33 = 100 g. 5% ointment: 12 x 33.33 = 400 g.
  • Check: (100 x 0.20) + (400 x 0.05) = 20 + 20 = 40 g of drug in 500 g = 8%. Correct.

Always run that last check. It takes five seconds and catches a swapped ratio.

5. Weight-based dosing

The rule. Dose per kg per day, then divide by the number of doses, then convert to a volume using the product's concentration.

Worked example. An 18 kg child needs 40 mg/kg/day in three divided doses. The suspension is 250 mg per 5 mL. What volume per dose?

  • Daily dose: 40 mg/kg x 18 kg = 720 mg/day.
  • Per dose: 720 / 3 = 240 mg.
  • Concentration: 250 mg per 5 mL = 50 mg per mL.
  • Volume: 240 / 50 = 4.8 mL per dose.

The trap: giving the daily dose as if it were a single dose. Read "divided" carefully.

6. Infusion rates

The rule. Work in one unit of time. For drop rates: mL per minute x the giving set's drop factor.

Worked example A. One litre over 8 hours through a set delivering 15 drops per mL.

  • 1000 mL / 8 h = 125 mL per hour.
  • 125 / 60 = 2.08 mL per minute.
  • 2.08 x 15 = 31.25, so 31 drops per minute.

Worked example B. A 70 kg patient needs 5 micrograms/kg/min from a bag holding 400 mg in 250 mL.

  • Dose: 5 x 70 = 350 micrograms per minute.
  • Per hour: 350 x 60 = 21,000 micrograms = 21 mg per hour.
  • Bag concentration: 400 mg / 250 mL = 1.6 mg per mL.
  • Rate: 21 / 1.6 = 13.1 mL per hour.

7. Creatinine clearance

The rule. Cockcroft-Gault: CrCl = ((140 - age) x weight in kg) / (72 x serum creatinine in mg/dL), multiplied by 0.85 for a female patient.

Worked example. A 68-year-old woman, 60 kg, serum creatinine 1.2 mg/dL.

  • 140 - 68 = 72.
  • 72 x 60 = 4,320.
  • 72 x 1.2 = 86.4.
  • 4,320 / 86.4 = 50 mL/min.
  • Female: 50 x 0.85 = 42.5 mL/min.

If creatinine is given in micromol/L, divide by 88.4 to get mg/dL first. State which weight you used when the patient is obese or underweight, because the choice changes the answer.
