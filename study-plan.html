An SPLE study plan built on the blueprint

The exam tells you exactly how it is weighted. Spend your time the same way, and let a diagnostic show you where to start.

Take the free diagnostic first

The exam in brief

  • 200 multiple-choice questions, each with four options, in two parts of 100.
  • 120 minutes per part, with a scheduled 30-minute break between them. That is 72 seconds a question.
  • Up to 10% of questions are unscored pilot questions, and you will not know which.
  • Some questions test knowledge; others give a scenario and test interpretation, analysis and decisions.
  • The passing score is 536 on a scale of 200 to 800. Results come 2 to 6 weeks after the testing window closes, with a feedback report comparing you to other candidates.
  • The September 2025 guide allows up to four attempts a year, and two further attempts after passing to improve your score for residency selection. Check your own eligibility with SCFHS.

Split your time by weight

For every 100 hours you study, spend them roughly like this. Each section can vary by up to 5% on the day.

  • Basic Biomedical Sciences10 hours
  • Pharmaceutical Sciences35 hours
  • Social, Behavioral and Administrative Sciences20 hours
  • Clinical Sciences35 hours

Pharmaceutical and Clinical Sciences together are 70% of the exam. Social and Administrative Sciences, at 20%, includes biostatistics, pharmacoeconomics, ethics and Saudi pharmacy law, which many candidates leave too late.

A suggested 8-week plan

Adjust it to your diagnostic result: move your weakest domain earlier and give it more time.

  1. 1

    Week 1

    Take the diagnostic. Then pharmacology by drug class: mechanisms, indications, adverse effects (2.2).

  2. 2

    Week 2

    Toxicology and antidotes, drug interactions, medicinal chemistry basics (2.1, 2.2).

  3. 3

    Week 3

    Pharmaceutics, pharmacokinetics and calculations, with daily drills from the seven calculation patterns (2.4, 2.5, 2.6).

  4. 4

    Week 4

    Therapeutics of common conditions, patient assessment and lab values (4.5, 4.6).

  5. 5

    Week 5

    Therapeutic drug monitoring, pharmacogenomics, prevention and immunization, drug information (4.1 to 4.4).

  6. 6

    Week 6

    Biostatistics, pharmacoeconomics, ethics, medication safety, and the Saudi health system and pharmacy law (3.1 to 3.10).

  7. 7

    Week 7

    Basic biomedical sciences (1.1 to 1.4), then back to your weakest domain.

  8. 8

    Week 8

    Timed mixed sets at 72 seconds a question, review every miss, take the official SCFHS practice exam, and rest before the day.

Numbers in brackets are the competency codes in the SCFHS blueprint.

Saudi-specific sources on the official list

The SCFHS reference list includes local sources that general review books do not cover:

  • The SFDA drug list
  • The SFDA procedures and controls for narcotic and psychotropic substances (published in Arabic)
  • The Ministry of Health list of required immunizations

On exam day

  • Bring your scheduling permit, on paper or on your phone, and an unexpired passport or national or residence identity card. Your name must match the permit exactly.
  • Arrive early. Arriving more than 30 minutes after your scheduled time means you will not be admitted, and it counts as an attempt unless the committee accepts a documented reason.
  • Phones and every other electronic device are prohibited in the testing area, even switched off. You are given writing surfaces for notes and calculations.
  • You sign out and back in for every break. Overrunning the break comes out of your next part's time.
  • Exam content is confidential. Candidates may not reproduce questions or discuss them with anyone, including preparation groups, before or after the exam.

Source for every fact on this page: SCFHS, Saudi Pharmacist Licensure Examination Examination Content Guideline, September 2025. Rules change; check the current guide before you book.
